## Why does "Buy" take me to eBay or Etsy?

Because that's where the actual transaction happens. ELM Vintage is the showroom — the place where I photograph, write about, and curate the pieces. eBay and Etsy handle the cart, the payment, and the shipping. When you click "Buy," you'll land on that piece's listing on whichever platform fits best. You're buying from me either way.

## How do you ship?

I ship from my home studio in the United States, US-only for now. Shipping is calculated at checkout based on your zip code and the weight of the piece — eBay and Etsy do the math for you, so you'll see the exact cost before you pay.

Vintage costume jewelry usually ships in a small padded box; ceramics get more cushioning. I aim to have your piece in the mail within two business days of payment.

## What's your return policy?

30 days, buyer pays return shipping. If the piece isn't right for you, message me through eBay or Etsy and I'll get you the return address. As long as it comes back in the condition it left in, I'll refund the full purchase price.

## Are these pieces authentic? How do you identify makers and eras?

I identify each piece to the best of my ability, using hallmarks, materials, construction style, and what I've learned from a lot of years of looking. Where I'm confident, I'll attribute a maker (Trifari, Coro, Weiss, Eisenberg, etc.). Where I'm not, I'll say so — "appears to be Mid-Century, unsigned" or "possibly Trifari." Pieces are sold as described.

If you collect a specific maker and want a closer look at a hallmark before buying, [message me about it](#) and I'll take a clearer photo.

## How do I take care of vintage costume jewelry?

Costume jewelry is more delicate than fine jewelry. A few simple rules:

- **Don't shower or swim in it.** Water is rough on foil-backed rhinestones and on most plated metals.
- **Don't spray perfume directly on it.** Spritz first, then put the jewelry on.
- **Store flat in a soft pouch or lined drawer**, not in a tangled jewelry box. Stones can scratch each other.
- **Polish with a dry soft cloth** if a piece looks dull. Skip the silver-polish dips on costume pieces — they can strip plating and damage stones.

If you have a specific question about a piece you bought, message me — I'm happy to help.

## How do I care for the ceramic pieces?

The ornaments, pins, and magnets I make are real fired ceramic — sturdy, but still ceramic, so a few simple rules apply:

- **Don't put them in the dishwasher.** None of them need it.
- **Clean with a damp cloth.** Don't soak.
- **Handle them gently.** Like any ceramic, they can chip if dropped — the thinner sections on pins and ornaments are the most vulnerable spot.
- **Magnets**: keep them off polished surfaces if the back has any rough spots — better safe than scratched.

If anything chips, breaks, or arrives damaged, message me and I'll make it right.

Maybe! Vintage is one-of-a-kind by definition, but I'm always hunting, and I source by era and style. The best move is to [join the newsletter](#) — when something similar comes in, you'll see it before anyone else.

## Do you take custom orders?

For ceramics, no — the small things I make are what I make, when I make them. For vintage, also no — I sell what I find. If you're hunting for a specific kind of piece, [tell me](#) and I'll keep it in mind on my next sourcing trip, but I can't promise.

Not yet. ELM Vintage is US-only for now. I'll announce it on the newsletter the day that changes.
